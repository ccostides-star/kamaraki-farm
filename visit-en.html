Our area

Kymi and the villages of Evia.

Kymi is a small town in eastern Evia, high above the Aegean. Ferries from its harbour sail to Skyros, Skopelos and Alonissos, and the old 19th-century steam mill still stands on the seafront.

History & Culture

A corner with deep roots.

Kymi was the birthplace of Dr George Papanikolaou, and his house in town is now a museum. The Folklore Museum of Kymi is considered one of the best in Evia. The old stone bridge that gave the farm its name is part of this same landscape.

Activities

What to do in the area.

Make a day of it. Combine your farm visit with Kymi and its coast.

Sea

Beaches & Swimming

Paralia Kymis and Platana are the main seafronts. Agios Georgios and Soutsini are good beaches nearby. For something wilder, head north-east to Tsilaros and Thapsa, Evia’s “blue lagoon”. Both are reached by roads that are partly unpaved, so check conditions before you set off. Thapsa is a protected Natura area.

Nature

Hiking, Climbing & Exploration

The trails around Kymi suit every season. One of the loveliest starts at Chonefitiko, with coffee under the trees and water from the spring, and in summer you can hike to the waterfalls at Manikia. Most trails also work for cycling. Manikia is also one of Greece’s best-known climbing areas, busy through the winter too. Stop by the farm for fresh food before or after a day on the rock.

Taste

Gastronomy & Local Products

Kymi is famous for its figs, and Kymi dried figs carry a Protected Designation of Origin (PDO). Ask for askada, the local name for two dried figs pressed together. In Platana, a seaside village with charming tavernas, you can have fish and mezze. From our farm, take home what suits your table: seasonal vegetables, olive oil, fig jam or chutney.

Tradition

Tradition & Museums

The Folklore Museum of Kymi is considered one of the best in Evia, and an annex is housed in the Chrysanthopoulos mansion in Pyrgos. In town you can visit the house of George Papanikolaou, who was born in Kymi, and on the seafront the old steam mill, built in the late 19th century. The old stone bridge that gave the farm its name is part of the same landscape. Check opening hours before you go.
